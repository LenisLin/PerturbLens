# Scientific Standards

## Fact, inference, and proposal

Every scientific statement must be identifiable as one of:

- **Fact**: directly supported by a source or validated result.
- **Inference**: interpretation supported by evidence but not directly measured.
- **Proposal/Hypothesis**: a planned analysis or testable explanation.

## Core claim boundaries

- Similar response vectors do not establish causal mechanism equivalence.
- Prediction failure does not prove the response is mathematically unpredictable.
- A state embedding is a representation, not the complete cellular state.
- Cross-readout association does not establish causal direction between molecular and morphological changes.
- A combination residual is relative to its declared null and is not automatically biological synergy.
- SystemaResidual emphasizes perturbation-specific structure relative to its reference pool; it does not define a corrected biological truth.

## Statistical principles

- The inference unit must match the intended generalization unit.
- Cells, technical replicates, perturbation instances, targets, compounds, contexts, and experiments are not interchangeable replicates.
- Coverage and exclusions accompany summary metrics.
- Multiple testing and hierarchical pooling are specified before confirmatory inference.
- Common-support comparisons are preferred when comparing representations or models.
- Negative/null findings are retained when the comparison is adequately powered and validated.
